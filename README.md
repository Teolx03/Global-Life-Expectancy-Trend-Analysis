# Global Life Expectancy Trend Analysis (R)

**An exploratory data analysis project examining global life expectancy trends using R and World Health Organization (WHO) data.**

## Project Overview

This university group project explores global life expectancy trends from 2000 to 2021, examining differences across countries, regions, genders, and age groups using data from the World Health Organization (WHO).

The project also investigates the relationship between life expectancy and economic conditions by integrating GDP per capita data from the World Bank.

The analysis involves data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, statistical modelling, and data visualization using R.

**Project Type:** University Group Project  
**Programming Language:** R  
**Tools & Libraries:** ggplot2, dplyr, tidyr, tidyverse, WDI  
**Key Skills:** Data Cleaning, Data Visualization, Statistical Analysis, Linear Regression, Correlation Analysis

---

## My Contributions — Japan Life Expectancy Analysis

I was responsible for **Part 1.2: Country-Level Life Expectancy Analysis and Five-Year Prediction**, focusing on Japan.

My contributions included:

- **Country Identification:** Identified Japan as having the highest recorded female life expectancy at birth (87.2 years) in the 2021 dataset.
- **Trend Analysis:** Examined Japan's historical life expectancy trends from 2000 to 2021, comparing life expectancy indicators at birth and at age 60.
- **Gender Comparison:** Compared male and female life expectancy trends, identifying consistently higher life expectancy among females.
- **Data Visualization:** Created line charts using R and ggplot2 to visualize historical trends and differences across indicators and genders.
- **Predictive Modelling:** Applied linear regression to estimate female life expectancy at birth over the following five years, producing a model-based estimate of approximately 87.9 years for 2026.

This analysis strengthened my practical skills in data exploration, comparative analysis, statistical modelling, and data-driven interpretation.

## Overall Group Project

The complete group project consisted of four analytical components:

| Section | Description |
|---|---|
| Part 1.1 | Global and regional life expectancy trends |
| **Part 1.2** | **Japan life expectancy analysis and five-year prediction (My Contribution)** |
| Part 1.3 | Top 10 countries with the highest average annual increases in life expectancy |
| Part 2 | Relationship between GDP per capita and life expectancy |

As part of the broader group analysis, WHO life expectancy data were integrated with World Bank economic indicators to investigate how economic conditions relate to population health outcomes.

The group's GDP analysis reported:

- **Pearson Correlation Coefficient:** 0.83
- **R-squared:** Approximately 0.68

These results indicate a positive association between GDP per capita and life expectancy within the aggregated dataset, rather than establishing a causal relationship.

## Limitations

- The analysis uses historical data from 2000 to 2021.
- The five-year life expectancy estimates are based on linear regression and assume that historical trends continue.
- The broader group's GDP analysis examines statistical associations and does not establish causality.
- The repository provides the original group report rather than a fully reproducible analysis environment.
